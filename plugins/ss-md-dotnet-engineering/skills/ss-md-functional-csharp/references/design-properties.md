## Recognize and preserve established design properties

- **Activate on intent, evidence, or a diagnosed need.** Use explicit requirements, observable contracts and surrounding patterns, or a concrete problem identified during Clean Code, DDD, refactoring, architecture, or performance work. A lambda, record, static method, or LINQ expression alone does not establish a functional design or justify a rewrite.
- **Inspect the affected boundary.** Read relevant callers, implementation, tests, and existing decisions. Identify what actually holds: which inputs remain unchanged, which facts are explicit, whether repeated calls observe external state, what results mean, and where effects occur. Distinguish an intended contract from an incidental implementation detail; state material uncertainty rather than invent a purity guarantee.
- **Preserve properties more strongly than adopting techniques.** Do not add clock reads, database writes, messages, input mutation, or mutable captures to a calculation relied upon as pure. Obtain facts and perform effects at an appropriate orchestration boundary while preserving transactions and concurrency checks. If the requirement genuinely needs a changed contract, make that intentional change explicit and verify affected callers.
- **Preserve the outcome and evaluation contract.** Retain distinctions between absence, rejection, exceptions, and cancellation; preserve error accumulation versus fail-fast behavior. Keep deferred evaluation, snapshot semantics, ordering, invocation count, and resource lifetime where callers depend on them.
- **Use evidence proportionately.** Protect non-mutating inputs and significant effects with relevant tests; inspect types, captures, and call paths when tests cannot establish the property alone. Repeating a calculation twice with equal results does not prove purity or thread safety.
- **Allow a coherent mixed style.** Imperative orchestration, resource management, and private mutable working state can coexist with pure calculations. Protect the boundary and caller contract, not uniform syntax across every method.
- **Document durable decisions at the right level.** For a deliberately maintained subsystem, follow an existing architecture decision or scoped instruction that describes concrete constraints and their scope. Do not scatter "this is functional" comments, create documentation for every local calculation, or treat a style marker as proof.
- **Resolve priorities by responsibility.** Correctness, business invariants, contracts, resource ownership, and operational constraints come first. Preserve established design properties within those constraints; use clear intent and visible data flow to choose among compatible techniques. Resolve a real conflict explicitly rather than silently discarding either a required budget or a meaningful design boundary.
